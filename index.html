About me

I’m a textile engineer who builds a research career around sustainable materials. My B.Sc. at DUET finished with a 3.95/4.00 CGPA and first-class-first position, and I’m now completing my M.Sc. there.

My work spans electrospun nanofibers for wound care, activated carbon from jute sticks, metal–organic frameworks made from textile sludge and PET waste, and hybrid ring-spun yarns for stretch fabrics.

I want to become a dedicated researcher and educator who makes a positive impact through science and innovation.

Short bio

Md. Likunuzzaman is a Lecturer in the Department of AMMT at Shanto-Mariam University of Creative Technology, Bangladesh. He holds a B.Sc. in Textile Engineering from Dhaka University of Engineering & Technology (CGPA 3.95/4.00, first class first) and is completing his M.Sc. there. His research covers electrospun nanofibers for wound care, waste-derived materials for water treatment, and hybrid yarns for stretchable fabrics. His work has appeared in AATCC Journal of Research and at ICMIEE 2024 and ICMMPE 2026.

Journal article · AATCC Journal of Research 2025, 12(6)

Circular textiles: garment waste into bag mats

Post-consumer garment waste was mechanically recycled to fibers, blended 50:50 with polyester, carded and heat-pressed into mats, then sewn into a prototype bag.

  • 29.12 N breaking force
  • 23.96% elongation
  • 13.79 µm fiber diameter

doi:10.1177/24723444251392643

Cotton-nylon hybrid ring-spun yarn

Cotton-nylon yarns at 80:20 and 90:10 blends in 16s to 40s counts, tested for evenness, imperfections, strength and elongation for stylish fabric.

Cotton-nylon-lycra yarn for stretch textiles

Core-spun hybrid yarns with 40D and 70D lycra in 16s and 20s counts. Lycra raised maximum force and elongation, with a small loss in evenness and count strength product.

  • Cotton-nylon 80:20
  • 40D and 70D lycra
  • Lower hairiness
